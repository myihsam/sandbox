How can IH consultants connect lab results to report-ready field records?

A practical workflow for matching laboratory results to sample IDs, reviewing field context and using myIH imports and exports to reduce re-entry.

myIHField documentation series · 3 min read

Direct answer: Connect IH laboratory results to field records through a consistent sample ID, then review the analyte, units, sampling information and laboratory qualifications before reporting. myIH supports importing a laboratory results file into the survey and exporting sample information with question responses and daily logs, reducing repeated assembly work.

Where does the field-to-report handoff break down?

The laboratory report arrives, but the report writer still has to find the corresponding field sheet, confirm which worker the sample represents and locate a note about an interrupted pump. The bottleneck is the relationship between records.

A useful handoff starts before the sample leaves the site. Keep the sample identifier consistent across the field record, laboratory submission and returned results. If the laboratory assigns a different identifier, preserve the relationship between the two rather than relying on row order or an employee name alone.

What should happen before results are treated as report-ready?

Use a deliberate reconciliation step. This suggested workflow separates file handling from technical review:

  1. Match records. Confirm that each result belongs to the intended sample and project; investigate unmatched or duplicate entries.
  2. Check the result description. Confirm the analyte, reported units and any laboratory qualifiers.
  3. Revisit collection details. Review the timing, applicable sampling information and recorded exceptions.
  4. Check the comparison basis. Confirm the selected exposure limit and averaging period are appropriate to the result being interpreted.
  5. Resolve questions. Record outstanding issues for clarification before drawing conclusions.

These are review activities for the consultant, not a claim that myIH automatically performs every check. OSHA’s validation guidance specifically recommends questioning results that do not fit site knowledge and consulting the laboratory when analytical error is suspected. Source: OSHA, Data Validation and Interpretation.

Does myIH receive lab results automatically?

The consultant page describes a file-import workflow: the user imports the laboratory results file, and myIH maps it into the survey. It also supports entering results manually. “Autoload” should therefore be understood as an import workflow, not an unattended live connection to every laboratory.

That distinction matters when evaluating software. A useful demonstration should show your team how it prepares a file, connects results to existing sample records and checks the resulting survey. Ask to walk through an example in which identifiers or units need attention.

What does the export contribute to report preparation?

myIH compiles sample tables, question-group responses and time-stamped daily logs into formatted Excel and PDF summaries. This brings the collected information together for report preparation without requiring the writer to recreate every table and field entry.

For example, a reviewer investigating a sampling interruption can consult the associated log and responses while reviewing the sample information. The benefit is less reconstruction of the field day. A formatted export is still an input to professional review; it does not by itself establish the validity of a sample or complete the consultant’s interpretation.

Common questions

Does importing results validate the sampling data?

No. Importing moves information into the survey. The consultant must still review identifiers, units, collection circumstances and laboratory qualifications before interpreting results.

Does myIH write the final IH report automatically?

The verified consultant workflow provides Excel and PDF summaries of sample information, prompted responses and daily logs. The hygienist still supplies the interpretation, conclusions and recommendations.
