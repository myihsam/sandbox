What should IH field notes capture alongside an air sample?

Keep task timing, controls and sampling events connected to air-sampling results with structured field notes and myIH’s tagged daily log.

myIHField documentation series · 3 min read

Direct answer: Industrial hygiene field notes should connect each air sample to the work being evaluated: the worker or location, task timing, operating conditions, controls and events that could affect interpretation. Keeping those observations with the sample helps a reviewer explain what a result represents and where its limitations begin.

Why is a laboratory result only part of the sampling record?

A concentration tells the consultant what was measured in a collected sample. The field record explains the work behind that measurement. At report time, the practical question is often specific: was this employee grinding throughout the sampling period, or moving between grinding, setup and cleanup?

A note that simply says “normal operations” leaves that question open. A useful record identifies what the observer actually saw, when it happened and which sample it concerns. OSHA’s personal air-sampling guidance calls for complete field notes, pump checks and calibration records. Source: OSHA Technical Manual, Section II, Chapter 1.

How can consultants record context without writing the report twice?

Use a consistent sampling record for the planned work and a time-stamped daily log for changes during the day. The following is a suggested documentation structure, to be adapted to the method and project:

  • Identity: client, project, sample ID and worker or sampling location.
  • Work: task, material, equipment and the relevant operating conditions.
  • Timeline: sampling start and stop, task changes and interruptions.
  • Controls: the control observed, its condition and any change during sampling.
  • Follow-up: unresolved details and who can clarify them.

Separate an observed condition from a reported explanation. “Operator said the extraction system stopped at 10:00” is different evidence from personally observing the stoppage.

What does a useful field note look like?

Illustrative example—not customer data: “10:20 — Sample AIR-014: operator moved from enclosed grinding station to open-bench cleanup. Local extraction was operating at the grinding station; no local extraction observed at the cleanup bench. Cleanup continued until 10:35.”

This note gives the reviewer a task transition, a time interval and a control observation tied to a sample. It does not prove that cleanup caused a particular concentration. It identifies a circumstance the hygienist can consider when interpreting the result or planning follow-up monitoring.

Where does myIH fit?

myIH brings prompted questions, sample records and a tagged daily log into the same field workflow. The consultant can record task-specific answers and associate observations with the relevant record while the work is happening. Question responses and time-stamped log entries can then accompany sample information in Excel and PDF summaries.

The value is continuity: the report writer can work from the recorded context instead of trying to match a result to a separate notebook entry after the visit. The hygienist still evaluates the evidence and writes the conclusions.

Common questions

Can field notes explain differences between two air samples?

They can identify differences in tasks, timing and controls that deserve investigation. Field notes support interpretation; they do not establish causation by themselves.

Does myIH replace the sampling method?

No. myIH organizes sampling information and field observations. The hygienist still selects and follows the appropriate sampling method.
